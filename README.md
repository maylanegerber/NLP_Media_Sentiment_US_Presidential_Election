## Abstract
With the media continuously playing a crucial role in shaping public discourse, over the course of recent elections, there has been an uptick in concern regarding whether news coverage remains truly neutral and unbiased. In an environment where each candidate can have the media turn on them in an instant, media bias has been widely researched in the context of how the public perceives them as a credible source of information. Few have taken a multifaceted approach to this process, instead focusing on public perception on a source or what an outlet is reporting. Seeking to provide insight on the intersection between news outlets and rhetoric over the course of elections, this research explores media sentiment within a source and in comparison to others to uncover how various outlets portray presidential candidates. Given that hundreds of millions of individuals receive information from the media regarding politics, I believe it is crucial to explore the changing discourse of such outlets over the course of the election through Natural Language Processing techniques. Focusing on the 2016 United States Presidential Election, I expect to find that different outlets would paint Trump and Clinton in divergent lights which could potentially influence voters' perceptions prior to such a critical election. By focusing on the use of language, framing of words, and sentiment of new outlets’ reporting, this study aims to uncover valuable insights for future presidential candidates and others in association with their campaign to help them further develop an understanding of how media may be leveraged in the future. 

## Discussion
With the upcoming 2024 election, understanding the potential bias in the media is crucial in determining how opposing candidates' portrayal by varying news outlets could play a role in influencing public opinion. the study specifically aimed at understanding the highly polarized 2016 United States Presidential election with a focus on the three political debates and post-election coverage. 
the word cloud and frequency analysis highlighted that besides the dominant feature of the 2016 presidential candidates, word choice or theme, no matter the event the articles were covering, tended to vary depending on the outlet. While this could indicate that news outlets tend to focus on varying points when they were covering election events, the fact that debate themes typically never showed up in the top ten most frequently used words besides in the Wall Street Journal suggests that the media may have already had an idea of what they wanted to portray regardless of topic. It would be interesting to further investigate this by looking at articles leading up to the first debate as well as months following the election rather than just focusing on the day after. 

The word specific sentiment and objectivity analysis showcased an interesting phenomenon with news sources generally becoming more positive as the election came to an end. This was in-line with the world cloud analysis, and could point towards more objective reporting as more definitive outcomes were present. Although, when looking at how each news source varied as the course of the election was carried out, I did take note that BBC seemed to display high subjectivity scores, which I found surprising. Given it is a news outlet outside of the United States, I had expected a more neutral reporting style and sentiment. BBC often having more negative or positive sentiment than the other chosen news sources could be a point to further investigate in the future along with other international news outlets. On the other hand, the United States news sources, besides Fox News, seemingly were more neutral with their reporting throughout the election. Fox News’ slow decline in objectivity should indicate their tendency to perhaps show favor of a particular candidate. I would recommend for campaign parties to look into this in the future to understand how Fox News or even BBC may be painting their candidate. By understanding the analysis in conjunction with how the public is receiving that news and thus voting, it could provide vital information to try to either fit that new platforms’ narrative or be prepared for the bias. 
the analysis of themes and coherence scores warranted similar results, with us ultimately concluding that news sources do tend to be subjective and perhaps have certain editorial stances prior to truly taking in the details of the debates. This was further backed-up by the increasing objectivity and neutrality of the post-election articles across all sources, as there may have been a shift to mere discussion of facts. It would be interesting to expand this research to cover more news outlets both within and outside of the U.S. On top of this, looking at elections with an incumbent could also provide more insight into how news sources may favor the current president prior to them running for reelection. Beyond this, I would be curious to explore more sentiment analysis techniques regarding direct quotations. Given pure sentiment scores would not provide insight into how the quote is being used to portray a candidate, looking more at news outlets’ choices regarding how they quote the presidential candidates would give more information on how the media may be biased. 

Overall, the 2016 United States Presidential Election was littered with controversy and scandal. Political candidates were constantly being portrayed in the news and on social media by the public in varying lights. By utilizing various NLP techniques, the political campaign teams in the future could leverage the information regarding election portrayal to enhance their campaign effectiveness. Media strategies could focus on their candidates tailoring their messages or discussing topics utilizing certain rhetoric that is typical of a particular news source in order to try to align more heavily with their narrative. On top of this, news outlets that already have a history of favoring candidates of the same party could be an indication they would be more perceptive to helping them engage with the public and promote the candidates goals once elected. This would allow candidates to connect to individuals who consume that news source, ultimately enhancing public support and reception in the face of other candidates or news outlets trying to craft a negative or false narrative.
